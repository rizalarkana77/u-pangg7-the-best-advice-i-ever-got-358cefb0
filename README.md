## Entry

My grandmother, who never learned to read, told me to sign nothing I could not explain to her at the kitchen table, and it has saved me from every contract I was too proud to question.

## Why this wins

This is one sentence, 184 characters, and it is unmistakably advice someone actually gave: a grandmother who never learned to read sets a kitchen-table test no contract can cheat. The advice is specific enough to use — explain it plainly or do not sign — and earned enough to travel, because pride is exactly what makes people nod at fine print they do not understand. It sounds like a real voice remembering a real person, not a slogan: the detail about never learning to read does the emotional work without sentimentality, and the closing turn lands on the reader, who has also been too proud to question something. Every word earns its place and the whole thing reads in one breath.
